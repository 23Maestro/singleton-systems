## Checked source patterns

The [official Cold Outreach download](https://www.acquisition.com/hubfs/%24100M%20Leads%20Cold%20Outreach%20Downloads.pdf?hsLang=en) contains several distinct scripts:

| Pattern | Source | When it fits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Goal question | Page 5: "Are you still looking to [4 word desire]?" | Reopen a goal question. Use the person's known goal. The source drops "still" on first contact; it does not require a prior reply. |
| Concrete preview | Page 10: a site observation leads to a prepared video. | A finished sample can make an idea clear. Claim only what our sample shows. |
| Same-thread follow-up | Pages 11–13: a sequence returns to the goal and next step. | Follow a real earlier message. Reuse its context and update old dates. |
| Unfinished booking | Page 3: "hey, any issues?" follows a calendar link. | The person has reached the booking step. Cold silence does not establish this state. |

These are Acquisition.com training examples. They are not proof of a tested result for Jerami. The download also has links, call requests and a longer contact sequence. The permission-first sample and short sequence are local options. Use [stages and dates](stages-and-dates.md) for the selected treatment and later plans.

The [official Warm Outreach download](https://www.acquisition.com/files/%24100M-leads-warm-outreach-downloads.pdf), pages 1–3, starts from a person who responded. It asks what drew their interest, then connects help to their answer. Use that discovery move after a reply. An unopened or unanswered email supplies no such answer.

## Choose the next message

These are our application rules and examples, not source quotes or approved email copy.

| State | Useful next move | Example of the move |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No reply to a sample offer | Keep the offer clear with enough context to recognize it. | Refer to the finished sample and the pending permission ask. |
| Sample link already sent | Follow the shared work. Offer a resend only when that is the intended next step. | Ask about 1 change the sample makes. Do not imply they watched it. |
| Someone named a need, then went quiet | Return to that need with 1 easy question. | For a stated launch-editing need: ask whether help with the launch edit is still needed. |
| They asked to see the sample | Share the checked sample. | Send its link with 1 useful note. Do not ask for permission again. |
| An agreed step stalled | Ask about that step. | After an accepted booking invitation, ask whether choosing a time caused a problem. |
| The sample's topic is old | Use it as proof of the method. | Describe what the edit does; avoid language that makes the old event sound current. |

Keep the ask tied to the prior exchange. A first email can introduce the offer. A follow-up can be shorter because the thread already holds that context. Add a new claim only when it is true and useful; do not make more sample work just to justify contact.

If all we know is that a team publishes videos, editing help is an offer to test. Their workload, need for an editor and desire to outsource remain unknown. Do not use wording that implies they asked for help.

## Review before showing the draft

- Does it continue the right conversation and account for every actual send?
- Can the reader tell why this message matters and what answering would lead to?
- Does it keep the value of the work clear without repeating a whole pitch?
- Are age, permissions, delivery uncertainty and the next step accurate?
- Is the wording Jerami's, with our adaptation kept separate from source advice?
