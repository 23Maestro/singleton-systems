Use source IDs `stage-0` through `stage-9`. The main stage cards are on PDF
page 5. Read the listed constraint pages for the detail. Headcount is context;
it does not by itself prove a stage. The company can have offers at different
levels of maturity. Do not label each prospect with this business ladder.

## Diagnose

Read paid work, client flow, delivery load, team structure and margins where
available. Name what is measured, reported or unknown. Compare the constraint
and graduation test. Do not infer revenue, profit, employee count or spare
capacity from a website, codebase, subscription or folder of study material.

As of the build review, paid recurring editing is supported by the current
operations guide and client records. This rules out treating the whole company
as pre-sale. Stage 2 is a candidate if steady new clients are the constraint.
Stage 3 needs evidence that delivery load and teaching help are the constraint.
The exact stage remains unconfirmed until those facts are known.

Consulting has internal proof and unpaid work; these do not prove a paid sale.
Describe its offer maturity separately. Do not assign the whole company Stage 0
because consulting is early. Do not claim consulting has no paid sales solely
because a case study is missing.

## Use the smallest next step

For an editing acquisition gap, review which recent paid clients fit best and
which source brought them. For a delivery gap, find the repeat task that blocks
the next approved edit. For consulting, clarify 1 live work problem and its
test before proposing a build. These are our adaptations, not stage rules.

Ask only for missing facts that change the diagnosis. A stage is a dated
assessment with source links and confidence. Keep it in the existing business
record or task when asked; never save it as private plugin state.
