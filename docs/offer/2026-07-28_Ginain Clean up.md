## The AI observation

**J:** Here's the interesting thing about how AI works. These companies
optimized on business, marketing, and human behavior.

What I've realized is AI is built to give you the solution *right now*, all of
it. You come into the chat, you ask a question — I could ask a 15-word question
and it gives me 300 words back, thinking I need all those details in that
second. I've gotten to the point where it overwhelms me. It's too much
information.

These things are powerful enough now that you can set it up so it isn't like
that. And that connects to what you're doing four times a month — really digging
into people, asking what their brand story is, who they are, what makes them
tick, what makes them happy. Really interviewing people on what it is and why.

That's important, and it's what I'm actively doing in my own operations right
now — establishing this for myself, for my own business, making myself more of
an operation. That's what's required with AI. And you can set it up that way
while doing it — log it, log the answers. Once AI knows all of that, that's the
only way it can be done well.

But people don't have time to do that in one swoop. We're busy.

So that's what I'm trying to work on: how can I build something that almost
*interviews* somebody, to get the answers out of them in an intuitive way they
might not manage on their own? And keep it friendly — not AI running wild trying
to overwhelm you with words. Because it's hard for it not to. It's so powerful
it doesn't know what to do with all that information, so it throws a bunch of
words at you. That's not beneficial. It needs to be palatable.

I'm not saying this is the grand plan. I'm just getting an idea out. It's an
interest of mine and it could be valuable later.
