# Clients and outreach

Supabase owns the client record. The dashboard shows it.
Notion is a past source. The full outreach loop stays in 23M-281 for later.

## One home per fact

| Need | Home |
| --- | --- |
| Name, Lane, relationship and short context | Supabase, shown in the dashboard |
| Sent messages and replies | Gmail or the original channel |
| Outreach events and results | The existing outreach sheet |
| Next action Jerami accepts | Asana, opened from the dashboard |
| Software build, tests and proof | Linear, linked to the client or offer |

The sheet may keep 500 rows without 500 client files or Asana tasks.
Reuse contact and opportunity IDs across owners.
Supabase may index the ledger for fast reads; it must not become a second
editable event history. Keep source IDs and show when a read is stale or fails.

## The client card

Show a short, linked detail panel:

- Who they are and why the connection matters.
- Lane and relationship, with a brief note such as “Prospect (friend).”
- Outreach stage, last real contact and its source link.
- Next step and review date, or why the work is dormant.
- Open the thread, draft, Asana task or related build.

Aim for 50–150 words; 500 is a ceiling. Keep deeper work behind links.
Do not create a file for each contact. Unknown dates stay unknown.
A saved draft is not a sent message.

## Keep the daily view small

Show current work and due reviews first. Keep dormant people searchable but
out of the daily queue. Add a task only when there is a chosen next action.
An old lead needs a new reason or an agreed date before it returns to the queue.

Relationship, outreach stage and payment are separate. Cold or warm describes
the route to a person. It does not prove buying interest. One company may have
several contacts and offers. Closing one opportunity must not close the others.
